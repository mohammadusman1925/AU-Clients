# PART 1: CONDITION PROFILES (Australian Context)

---

## 1. Hip Osteoarthritis

**Scale:** 2.1 million Australians (8.3%) have osteoarthritis. Chance increases considerably after age 45. 27% of people 45+ show radiographic evidence of hip OA.

**Cost to system:** Australia spent $4.3 billion on OA management in 2020-21.

**Surgery pipeline:** Over 51,000 hip replacements in 2019, projected to exceed 79,000 by 2030. Public hospital median wait: 152 days. Some states: 3+ years.

**Treatment pathway:** GP > imaging > physio referral > cortisone > wait for surgery. Conservative treatment is recommended first per RACGP guidelines but is routinely skipped.

**Ad relevance:** Largest addressable audience. OA is the #1 reason for hip replacement. Every other condition feeds into or overlaps with this.

> Sources: AIHW, RACGP clinical practice guidelines, AOANJRR annual reports

---

## 2. Cortisone Shots for Hip Pain

**How it works in Australia:** GP referral to specialist or radiologist. Ultrasound-guided injection. Most patients pay $150-$350 out of pocket. Some Medicare coverage via MBS items for imaging-guided injections.

**How long it lasts:** Only 33% still have pain relief at 3 months. 50% of patients get relief lasting 2 weeks or less. 20% get zero response.

**Limitations:** 3-4 shots per year maximum. Repeated doses damage cartilage and can cause avascular necrosis (bone death from blood supply loss).

**Forum language:**
- "Cortisone shot lasted approximately 90 days. After that, back to square one."
- "I got the steroid injection and it didn't help the pain at all."
- "In the 5 months since the injections, my bilateral hip pain is easily 20x worse than before."
- "Had a number of cortisone injections for hip bursitis that did not help beyond initially. No cure."

**Ad relevance:** Cortisone failure is a trigger event. This is where buyers are emotionally ready for an alternative. The "temporary fix" angle is backed by clinical evidence.

> Sources: Mayo Clinic Connect forums, HealthUnlocked Pain Concern, BoneSmart forums, Brisbane Hip Clinic

## 3. Sciatica and Hip Pain

**Scale:** 40% lifetime prevalence in Australia. 4 million Australians live with back problems. Piriformis syndrome (hip/buttock area) accounts for 0.3-6% of all sciatica cases.

**Hip connection:** Sciatica often presents as hip pain. Shooting pain down the leg from the hip/buttock area. Many patients with "hip pain" actually have sciatic nerve involvement. Piriformis syndrome mimics hip joint pain.

**Treatment pathway:** GP > imaging > physio > pain management > sometimes surgery. Public hospital physio wait times up to 12 months.

**Forum language:**
- "I've had chronic pain for three years due to sciatica, and I've tried everything to help relieve the pain."
- "Pain shoots down my leg"
- "Shooting pain down leg" (frequency: 38 mentions in VOC research)

**Ad relevance:** Sciatica sufferers are desperate for relief. They search for hip pain solutions because that's where they feel it. The belt's infrared + vibration combination targets the piriformis and surrounding tissue.

> Sources: ABS health data, AIHW, VOC research (200+ forum posts)

## 4. Hip Bursitis (Trochanteric Bursitis)

**Scale:** Incidence 1.8-5.6 per 1,000 people per year. Women have 2-5x higher risk than men (15% of women affected). Most prevalent in the 4th-6th decades of life (40s-60s).

**Nature:** Chronic condition with high recurrence. Inflammation of the bursa (fluid-filled sac) on the outside of the hip. Pain lying on the affected side, climbing stairs, walking.

**Treatment pathway:** Cortisone injections (temporary), physio, NSAIDs, sometimes surgical intervention for chronic cases. High recurrence rate makes ongoing management necessary.

**Forum language:**
- "Had a number of cortisone injections for hip bursitis that did not help beyond initially"
- "I can't sleep on my side" (the #1 complaint across all hip conditions)
- "Lying on the affected hip is like pressing on a bruise"

**Ad relevance:** Bursitis is chronic and recurring. Cortisone wears off. This positions the belt as ongoing daily management rather than a one-time fix. The "I can't sleep on my side" angle is the strongest emotional hook.

> Sources: HealthUnlocked, clinical incidence data, Australian physio guidelines

## 5. Prediabetic Neuropathy and Hip/Joint Pain

**Scale:** 13.7-16.7% of Australians have prediabetes. Of those, 11-25% exhibit peripheral neuropathy. 13-26% have neuropathic pain. Diabetic patients have nearly double the arthritis incidence (52% vs 27%).

**Hip connection:** Prediabetic neuropathy often affects hips, thighs, and buttocks. Proximal diabetic neuropathy specifically targets the hip and thigh area. Many prediabetic Australians don't know their nerve pain is connected to blood sugar.

**Treatment gap:** Most treatment focuses on blood sugar management, not the pain itself. Red light therapy has emerging evidence for diabetic neuropathy pain reduction.

**Ad relevance:** This is an underserved audience. They search for "hip pain" or "thigh pain" without connecting it to prediabetes. The belt targets the symptom they're actively searching for. Potential angle: "Your hip pain might not be your hip."

> Sources: ABS National Health Survey, Diabetes Australia, NCBI diabetic neuropathy studies

## 6. Hip Labral Tears

**Scale:** Found in 22-55% of patients with hip pain. But also present in 38-69% of asymptomatic people. 40-50% improve with conservative treatment alone. Only 22.9% eventually need surgery.

**Nature:** Tear in the cartilage ring around the hip socket. Common in active adults. Pain in the groin or front of hip. Clicking or catching sensation.

**Treatment pathway:** Often found on MRI when investigating hip pain. Conservative management (physio, activity modification) works for nearly half. Surgery only when conservative treatment fails.

**Ad relevance:** The stat that 40-50% improve with conservative treatment is a powerful positioning tool. "Before surgery, try conservative treatment. Almost half of labral tears improve without cutting."

> Sources: Clinical imaging studies, orthopaedic outcome data

## 7. Referred Back Pain to Hip

**Scale:** 71% of hip-spine syndrome patients report buttock pain. 57% report thigh pain. 55% report groin pain. Misdiagnosis is common and can lead to unnecessary surgery.

**The problem:** Lower back problems often present as hip pain. Hip problems often present as back pain. Patients get bounced between specialists. Some get hip surgery when the problem is their back, or vice versa.

**Ad relevance:** These patients are confused and frustrated. They've been told different things by different practitioners. The belt targets the hip area regardless of whether the source is the hip joint, the bursa, the piriformis, or referred pain from the spine. "Whether it's your hip or your back, this targets the pain where you feel it."

> Sources: Hip-spine syndrome clinical data, orthopaedic misdiagnosis studies

## 8. Sedentary Lifestyle / Desk Worker Hip Pain

**Scale:** 36.3% of Australians work from home (post-COVID). 67% of desk workers report hip discomfort. 85% of people sitting 6+ hours daily have hip flexor tightness.

**Nature:** Prolonged sitting tightens hip flexors, weakens glutes, creates anterior pelvic tilt. Pain in the front of the hip, groin, lower back. Worse when standing after sitting. Common in WFH Australians since COVID.

**Treatment:** Movement, stretching, standing desks, physio. But compliance is low. People know they should move more. They don't.

**Forum language:**
- "I sit at a desk all day and my hips are killing me"
- "Since working from home my hip pain has gotten so much worse"

**Ad relevance:** Growing audience. Younger end of target demographic (45-55). WFH angle is specifically Australian-relevant. Belt can be worn while working at desk. "20 minutes while you work. No appointment needed."

> Sources: ABS Work from Home data, workplace health studies

# PART 2: AUSTRALIAN CONTEXT

---

## Healthcare System Reality

- **Bulk billing decline:** Only 49.1% of patients fully bulk-billed in 2024-25 (down from 67.6% in 2020-21). Over 1.2 million Australians delayed seeing a GP due to cost.
- **Physio costs:** $80-$150 per session. Only 5 Medicare-subsidised sessions per year under chronic disease management plan. Then full price.
- **Public vs private:** Public hip replacement wait: 152 days median. Some states: 3+ years. Private: 28 days but $20K-$30K total cost. 85% of insured patients still pay out of pocket (avg $4,800 gap).
- **GP referral pathway:** To see a specialist, you need a GP referral. GP appointment: $40-80 gap if not bulk-billed. Specialist: $200-$500 for initial consultation.
- **Viscosupplementation:** Not listed on the Australian PBS. Patients pay full cost.

## Australian Cultural Context

- **Stoicism:** "She'll be right" culture. Toughing it out. Not wanting to complain. This means Australians present later, with worse symptoms, having endured longer.
- **Pain language:** "Crook" (sick/injured), "buggered" (broken), "dodgy" (unreliable body part), "stuffed" (exhausted/damaged). "My hip's buggered" is how they describe it to mates.
- **Activities that matter:** Walking is the most common exercise for older Australians. Gardening, golf, lawn bowls, cricket (watching or playing). Beach walks. Playing with grandkids. Gardening injuries led to 238,055 hospitalisations in 2022-23.
- **Trust signals:** "Engineered/Designed in Sydney" works. Australian-made preference is strong. TGA listing carries weight. "Not some cheap import" is a real concern.
- **Healthcare frustration:** Australians are increasingly frustrated with the public system. Wait times. GP shortage. Bulk billing decline. "The system is broken" is a common sentiment. This creates openness to self-managed solutions.

# PART 5: REAL OBJECTIONS

---

## Trust Objections

**1. "It's just a coloured light. How can that do anything?"**
- Created by: Cheap LED gadgets flooding Amazon/eBay with no therapeutic wavelength.
- Real belief: "If it looked like a medical device, I might believe it. But it looks like a gadget."
- Overcome: Specific wavelength claims (660nm + 850nm). "If it doesn't specify the wavelength, it's decoration. This is therapy."

**2. "If it actually worked, my doctor would have told me about it."**
- Created by: Authority bias. GPs recommend cortisone, physio, NSAIDs. Red light therapy isn't in the standard pathway yet.
- Real belief: "Things my doctor doesn't recommend aren't real medicine."
- Overcome: "Your doctor uses this wavelength in their laser therapy machine. This brings it home."

**3. "These devices claim to fix everything -- that's a red flag."**
- Created by: Marketing overreach across the category. Products claiming skin, weight loss, pain, mood, sleep all at once.
- Real belief: "A product that does everything does nothing."
- Overcome: Narrow the claim. "This does one thing: reduce hip pain and inflammation." Restraint IS credibility.

## Identity Objections

**4. "My partner/family will think I'm wasting money on another gimmick."**
- Created by: Previous failed purchases erode household credibility. Buying another device feels like admitting gullibility.
- Real belief: "I've already lost credibility with my family by buying things that didn't work."
- Overcome: "Used by physiotherapists and chiropractors." Make it feel medical, not impulsive.

**5. "I'm not the kind of person who falls for these things."**
- Created by: Self-image as practical, evidence-based, not easily fooled.
- Real belief: "Smart people don't buy therapy devices from Facebook ads."
- Overcome: "You've done your research. Here's what the research says." Lead with evidence, not emotion.

## Risk Objections

**6. "I've already spent hundreds on gadgets that didn't work."**
- Created by: TENS units, massage guns, copper bracelets, magnetic insoles. Financial pain on top of physical pain.
- Real belief: "I'll feel stupid if I waste money again."
- Overcome: Money-back guarantee + realistic timeline (not "instant cure") + specific-condition testimonials.

**7. "What if it makes my hip worse?"**
- Created by: Cortisone injections that made pain "20x worse." Treatment trauma is real.
- Real belief: "Treatment can make things worse. I've experienced it."
- Overcome: Safety profile of red light therapy. "Unlike cortisone, there's no risk of making it worse."

**8. "It's too expensive for something that might not work."**
- Created by: Price comparison against single-modality devices ($30-60).
- Real belief: "The risk-to-reward ratio isn't clear."
- Overcome: Cost-per-use framing. "One physio session or 365 days of therapy at home." Money-back guarantee removes risk entirely.
