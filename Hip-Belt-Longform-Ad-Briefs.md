# BRIEF 9: PERMANENT RESTRICTIONS

**ICP:** Active Australians 45-60 who still run, play sport, hike, or do physical work. Their hip is slowing them down but they're still pushing through. They've been told surgery is coming. Nobody has told them what surgery permanently bans them from doing. The word "permanently" changes everything.

**POV:** First person. Man, 52. Still plays social cricket on weekends. Runs 3 mornings a week (slower now, with a limp, but still goes). Hikes with his wife twice a month. His hip is getting worse. His surgeon said "we should talk about replacement." He said yes. Then he Googled "activities after hip replacement."

**Summary of the Story:** An active man who defines himself by what his body can do discovers that hip surgery permanently bans him from most of what he loves. No running -- ever. No jumping. No contact sports. No deep squatting. The "approved list" is walking, cycling, swimming, golf, doubles tennis. He looks at the list and sees a smaller version of himself. He decides to try everything else first, because once he has the surgery, the approved list is forever.

**Type:** Identity loss / "forever" confrontation

**Desire Type:** Desire to stay who he is. He doesn't want pain relief at the cost of his identity. He'd rather hurt and run than not hurt and walk.

**Awareness:** Solution-aware but restriction-unaware. He knows surgery fixes the joint. He doesn't know it permanently restricts what the joint can do. The "forever" restrictions are the gap.

**Concept:** The ad opens with a list. "After hip replacement, here's what you can do: walking, cycling, swimming, golf, doubles tennis." Pause. "Here's what you can NEVER do again: running, jumping, hiking rough terrain, contact sports, deep squats, heavy lifting." The contrast between the two lists is the ad. The belt enters as the thing that lets him keep both lists open. "While I can still run, I'm not giving it up for an approved list."

**Angle:** Permanent restrictions. Surgery doesn't just fix your hip. It permanently limits what you can do with it.

**Current Belief:**
- "After surgery I'll be back to normal. Better than normal."
- "The restrictions are just during recovery. Then you're free."
- "Modern hip replacements let you do everything."
- "I'll be back running in 6 months."

**Belief Shift:**
- FROM: "After recovery I'll do everything I used to do" > TO: "No running. No jumping. No contact sports. No deep squats. Those restrictions don't end at 14 weeks. They end when you do."
- FROM: "The restrictions are temporary" > TO: "PERMANENT. The approved list is: walking, cycling, swimming, golf, doubles tennis. That's it. Forever."

**Moments:**
