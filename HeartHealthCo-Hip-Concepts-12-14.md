## HIP ANGLE

### 12   The 30-minute drive

> **CORE MESSAGE**
> *"Why a short drive can feel harder than a long walk."*

> **PAIN POINT**
> Walking manages reasonably, but office chairs and car seats create deep discomfort around the hip or groin. Arriving home means having to pause before getting out of the car. Sitting becomes the real enemy.

**Story**
The person manages walking reasonably well, but office chairs and car seats create deep discomfort around the hip or groin. They arrive home and have to pause before getting out of the car. Introduce a portable routine that can be used after work or travel.

**Why it may work**
Driving and desk work are high-frequency triggers that competitors do not own strongly. People with hip impingement repeatedly identify sitting and driving as their biggest everyday problems.

## HIP ANGLE

### 13   The side-sleeper's impossible position

> **CORE MESSAGE**
> *"The bedtime pillow arrangement nobody understands until hip discomfort begins."*

> **PAIN POINT**
> Sleeping on the affected side creates pressure. The other side pulls across the hip. The back feels unnatural. Every night becomes a pillow-arrangement ritual between the knees and around the body, and sleep never feels restful.

**Story**
Sleeping on the affected side creates pressure. Sleeping on the other side may pull across the hip. Sleeping on the back feels unnatural. The person begins arranging pillows between the knees and around the body. Introduce a short pre-bed comfort routine that complements their existing sleep setup.

**Why it may work**
As with shoulder discomfort, sleep creates immediate urgency and a repeatable product ritual. Nighttime hip pain and side-sleeping difficulty appear throughout hip-pain communities.

## HIP ANGLE

### 14   The holiday pain tax

> **CORE MESSAGE**
> *"The part of the holiday photos never show."*

> **PAIN POINT**
> Looking fine in photos, but by evening the hip is throbbing and the next day's plans depend entirely on how it feels. The invisible cost of participating in life that nobody sees.

**Story**
The person completes the flight, drives to the beach and walks around sightseeing. They look fine in photos, but by evening the hip is throbbing and the next day's plans depend on how it feels. Position the product as a portable recovery routine for active days and travel.

**Why it may work**
This sells the ability to participate in life rather than simply promising relief. It is aspirational, emotional and particularly relevant to active adults who do not identify as disabled.
