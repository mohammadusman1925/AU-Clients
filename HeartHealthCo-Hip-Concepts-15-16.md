## HIP ANGLE

### 15   The good-day flare

> **CORE MESSAGE**
> *"A good day can become tomorrow's flare."*

> **PAIN POINT**
> A better day leads to walking farther or exercising harder. That evening or next morning, the discomfort returns with force. Fear builds that every activity will trigger another setback. The boom-and-bust cycle feels inescapable.

**Story**
The person has a better day and walks farther, exercises harder or completes more chores. That evening or the following morning, the discomfort returns. They begin worrying that every activity will create another setback. Present the product as a consistent post-activity routine, supported by a simple activity and comfort diary.

**Why it may work**
The boom-and-bust cycle is common in chronic pain communities. It creates a recurring use case and supports retention because the product is positioned as a routine, not a one-time rescue.

**Best formats**
*Two-day UGC sequence: good day versus next-morning flare.*

## HIP ANGLE

### 16   When socks become a 10-minute task

> **CORE MESSAGE**
> *"The small morning task that reveals how much hip movement has changed."*

> **PAIN POINT**
> Putting on socks, stepping into trousers, tying shoes, and getting into the car all take longer. A chair, grabber, or another person's help becomes necessary for basic morning dressing. Simple independence disappears.

**Story**
Putting on socks, stepping into trousers, tying shoes and getting into the car begin taking longer. The person may need a chair, grabber or another person's help. The desired endpoint is simple independence with dressing and leaving the house.

**Why it may work**
Like the shoulder bra angle, socks and shoes are concrete and instantly recognizable. Dressing difficulties generate substantial community discussion because they expose the practical cost of reduced hip movement.

**Best formats**
*Morning-routine UGC and mobility diary.*
