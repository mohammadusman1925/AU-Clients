**2. The Job**

**What this concept tests:**

A new emotional entry point. This ad is about shoulder pain, but the
door the reader walks through is BEING WATCHED. The concept tests
whether pain told through a RELATIONSHIP lens (he notices, she sees him
noticing) converts better than pain told through a personal-experience
lens (I can\'t reach, I can\'t sleep). The hypothesis is that the
partner\'s gaze amplifies the pain. It makes it real, witnessed,
undeniable. And that amplification creates a stronger action trigger
than self-reported pain alone.

**Hypothesis:**

A reader whose partner has commented on how carefully she moves, or who
has caught a partner watching her struggle, will feel this ad in a way
that no \"my shoulder hurts\" ad can match. The pain becomes a
relationship story, not a medical story. If the reader has ever said
\"I\'m fine\" while knowing her partner doesn\'t believe her, this ad
speaks to the gap between what she says and what he sees.

**3. Who\'s Reading**

**Target:** The Watched Partner

A woman aged 48-68 whose shoulder pain has changed her movement patterns
enough that someone close to her has noticed. She is not immobile. She
is CAREFUL. She reaches with the other hand. She opens doors
differently. She rolls out of bed instead of stretching. She does not
notice she is doing it. But he does.

**Emotional state:**

Vulnerable but hiding it. The pain is manageable. She CAN reach, she CAN
lift. But she has stopped doing things the natural way. The moment her
partner noticed, the pain crossed from private to shared, and that made
it worse. Not physically. Emotionally. Being watched makes her feel
fragile.

**Key difference from AD 2\'s reader:**

AD 2\'s reader is facing a MEDICAL crisis. This reader is facing an
EMOTIONAL one. Her shoulder works. Badly, carefully, with compensation.
But the real pain is seeing herself through her partner\'s worried eyes.

**Funnel:** Top of funnel. Problem-aware. The partner\'s observation may
be the trigger that makes her take action.

**4. The Angle**

**Pain cluster:** Relationship impact of chronic pain. The emotional
burden of being watched and seen as fragile.

**Specific pain in customer language:**

\"He said I do everything carefully now. I didn\'t even notice I was
doing it. But he did.\"

**What makes this angle different:**

AD 2 enters through surgery. HIP-NEW-16 enters through failed products.
HIP-NEW-11 enters through daily humiliation. THIS concept enters through
SOMEONE ELSE\'S observation. The reader is not told she is in pain. She
is told that someone she loves can SEE she is in pain. That is a
fundamentally different emotional experience.

**5. Three Headline Options**

**Option A: \"The Observation\"**

**Hook:** \"My husband said something last week that stopped me. \'You
do everything so carefully now.\'\"

**Hook type:** Intimate Confrontation

**Emotional job:** The quote-within-a-quote creates intimacy. The reader
is overhearing a private moment. \"Stopped me\" signals this was a
turning point. The husband\'s words are gentle (not \"what\'s wrong with
you\" but \"carefully\") which makes them MORE devastating. He is not
criticizing. He is observing. And his observation names what she has
been hiding.

**Option B: \"The Flinch\"**

**Hook:** \"I reached for a cup on the top shelf. My husband flinched.
He flinched before I did.\"

**Hook type:** Reversal + Intimate Detail

**Emotional job:** The physical reversal, he flinched before she did,
captures the entire relationship dynamic in one image. He is so attuned
to her pain that his body reacts before hers does. The reader whose
partner has ever visibly braced while watching them move will feel this
viscerally.

**Option C: \"The Watch\"**

**Hook:** \"He doesn\'t say anything anymore. He just watches. And I can
feel him watching.\"

**Hook type:** Silence + Emotional Tension

**Emotional job:** The progression from saying something to silent
watching is worse. It means he has given up asking if she is okay. \"I
can feel him watching\" places the reader inside the relationship
dynamic: she is performing normalcy, he is watching her perform it, and
both know what is happening.

**6. Body Direction**

**Story Shape:** Linear narrative, first-person. \~1,400-2,200 words.

**Recommended POV:** The woman narrator. She tells the story, but the
PARTNER is the emotional mirror. His reactions mark the story\'s turning
points.

**Key Scenes:**

**The Observation**

The moment he says it. The words. Her reaction. What she says back
(\"I\'m fine\") and what she feels. The gap between the response and the
truth.

**The Inventory**

She starts noticing what he has been seeing. The other hand. The doors.
The careful way she rolls out of bed. The mug from the top shelf. All
the compensations she had made unconsciously. An inventory of small
surrenders she did not know she was making.

**The Watching**

He watches her now. Not constantly, but she catches him. Taking the mug
off the shelf before she reaches for it. Opening the car door. The small
acts of accommodation that confirm she is being seen as fragile. Each
one lands like a verdict.

**The Silent Fight**

She tells him to stop. He says stop what. She says stop watching me like
that. He says like what. The argument that is not about the watching. It
is about the pain being real now because someone else confirmed it. The
fight ends without resolution because there is nothing to resolve. The
pain is the problem, not the watching.

**The Discovery**

She finds the belt through someone. Possibly a friend whose husband had
said something similar, possibly through Dr. Sime\'s content. The
discovery comes through the relationship channel. Someone who understood
because their partner had the same worried look.

**The Mechanism**

Delivered through understanding why the shoulder restriction developed.
The soft tissue explanation. The blood supply issue post-menopause. The
inflammation loop. Presented not as a sales pitch but as the thing that
finally made sense of what was happening.

**The Results**

Graduated recovery. The first change is not the pain. It is that he
stops watching. Not because he stopped caring, but because there is
nothing to watch. She reaches for something and he does not flinch. The
results are measured in relationship moments.

**The Echo**

She reaches for the mug. He does not move. She almost does not notice.
But she does. The echo of the old pattern, noticed only by its absence.
The most powerful transformation is the one that becomes invisible.

**7. The Mechanism**

**Name:** \"Deep Tissue Reset\" (adapted for shoulder)

**Current belief:**

\"My shoulder is just getting worse. This is permanent.\"

**Reframe:**

The shoulder pain is not structural deterioration. It is soft tissue
around the shoulder joint that has become inflamed and restricted. The
rotator cuff tendons are starved of blood supply, particularly
post-menopause. The tissue is stuck in an inflammation loop. This is not
your shoulder falling apart. This is tissue that nobody has been
treating properly.

**Why nothing worked:**

Anti-inflammatories quiet the alarm but do not fix the source. Physio
loads the angry tendons before they are ready. Cortisone numbs
temporarily but the restriction returns. Ice reduces inflammation on the
surface. None of these reach the deep tissue layer where the restriction
actually lives. They were not wrong. They were shallow.

**What actually works:**

Driving warmth and fresh blood directly into the starved tissue. Red
light (660nm) and near-infrared (850nm) penetrate to the tissue layer
where the restriction lives. Heat increases local circulation. Vibration
stimulates blood flow and loosens the tissue. Compression holds
everything against the joint so the modalities reach where they need to
go. Four things working together, not one thing working alone.

**New belief:**

\"It was not permanent. It was tissue nobody was treating. And now he
has stopped watching me move.\"

**9. Persona & Voice**

**Narrator:** New narrator. Not from any existing ad.

**Age:** 54-64

**Relationship:** Long-term marriage or partnership (20+ years)

**Voice:**

More emotionally exposed than other narrators. She is not telling a pain
story. She is telling a relationship story that happens to involve pain.
Vulnerability is the tone, not determination. She does not sound like
she is conquering anything. She sounds like she is admitting something
she has been carrying quietly.

**What she sounds like:**

A woman telling a friend something she has not told anyone else. Quiet.
Honest. A little raw. Not dramatic. Not clinical. The kind of voice that
makes you lean in because she is not performing. She is just saying what
happened.

**What she does NOT sound like:**

A testimonial. A medical patient. A product reviewer. A woman who has it
all figured out. She is not offering advice. She is sharing an
experience. The product enters her story. She does not enter a product
pitch.
