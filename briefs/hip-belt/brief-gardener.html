What This Ad Is About

This is a woman who gave up gardening. Then she gave up the full dog walk. Then she stopped getting in the car without a plan. She didn't announce any of it. She just quietly stopped doing things, one at a time, until she forgot she'd ever done them.

That's the truth this ad sits in. Not the pain itself — the slow giving-up.

But here's the thing: this is not a sad ad. You show the problem quickly, then spend most of the video on what came back. The garden. The dog. The car. The shoes. That's what UGC is — showing the outcome, not performing the pain.

One idea: She thought this was over for her. It wasn't.

Your Script Guide

This is NOT a script to memorise. Read it. Get the feeling. Then talk naturally, in your own words. Wander. Correct yourself. Trail off. That's what makes it real.

Your detail will be different, and better, because it's yours. The must-do is the Sydney claim and calling out that this isn't from Amazon. Everything else — say it your way.

The Bigger Claim — You Must Cover This

This section is mandatory. Every creator must cover this in their own words, naturally inside the story.

Your viewer has been burned before. She's bought things that did nothing. So somewhere in your video, show her you checked this one out.

The four things that matter:

  • Engineered and designed in Sydney — say it, own it, make it a big deal
  • Not from Amazon — this is not a cheap import. Call it out directly
  • 30-day money-back guarantee — "that's all you can lose. Thirty days."
  • 1-year warranty — "nobody puts a year's warranty on something that doesn't work"

Tone: like you're telling a friend "I actually checked this one out before I bought it."

Film your screen: scroll the website, show the warranty page, show "Designed in Sydney." Hold each screen steady for 5 seconds, brightness up, no reflections. These clips are gold.

The Emotional Flow

Quietly gave things up, one at a time → stopped even noticing what she'd lost → sceptical but curious → the little things coming back → back in the garden → relief, not excitement

This is not a celebration video. It's a relief video. The ending is quiet. She's back in the garden and that's enough.

How To Film This

Clothing

Wear light-coloured pants and a light-coloured shirt. Not dark. The belt is dark, and it needs to be visible against your clothing in every shot.

Think: light grey, cream, white, soft blue, beige. No black jeans, no dark tops.

The Shape Of The Video

The first thing we see: Your hands in the garden, pulling a weed or touching soil. NOT your face. Two seconds of this before your voice comes in.

Two rules for the opening:

  • Don't start on your face. Start on the thing, or on the movement
  • Don't say hello and don't introduce yourself. Come in as though we've walked in halfway through

Tell it (~20% of the video): What you stopped doing. Be specific. Not "my hip hurts" — the garden, the dog, the car door, the route through Woolies.

Show the product (~15%): Pick it up like something you use every day. Mention the four therapies. Show yourself wearing it on the couch with a cuppa. Mention it's designed in Sydney, not Amazon, year's warranty, thirty days to send it back.

What changed (~65%): Stack the "finally" moments. The car. The dog. Picking things up. The shoes. The couch. Back in the garden. This is the bulk of the video — the outcome, not the problem.

Tone

Think: telling a friend something you've been quietly putting up with. Not upset. Not dramatic. Just honest, a bit tired of it, and then genuinely surprised it came back.

Before and After Scenes

Same room. Same angle. Same clothes. Same distance. The viewer sees the change, not hears about it.

The belt must be visible in at least half the "after" shots — on you, or sitting in frame.

Making It Sound Like You And Not Like An Ad

  • Don't act the pain. By now she's used to it. She works around it and carries on. Underplay it every time
  • Slow down. Real people talk slower than actors and leave gaps. If it feels too slow, it's about right
  • Do the thing first, talk second. Kneel in the garden, then say the line. Not the other way round
  • Swap our words for your words. Keep the specifics — the garden, the car, the dog. Change everything around them into how you'd actually say it
  • Keep the first take. Stumbles, restarts, losing your thread — that's what we want. If you nail it cleanly, do another one worse
  • Talk to one person, not a camera. Pick someone you know and tell it to them
  • Don't land the ending. Trail off, shrug, look away. A clean confident finish is what makes something look like a commercial
  • No smiling at the end. That one thing gives it away more than anything else
If you get to the end and it felt a bit flat and unfinished, that's usually the take we want.
