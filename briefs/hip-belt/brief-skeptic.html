What This Ad Is About

This is the skeptic. The bloke who doesn't buy things based on marketing. He checks the company. He tests the wavelengths with a meter. He looks up the research. He needs proof before he tells anyone anything works.

The hook is the surgery — a $25,000 hip replacement that Medicare barely covers. That's the alternative. That's what he's trying to avoid. And this is the thing he found that actually checks out.

The energy is measured, methodical, dry. He walks through each therapy like he's reviewing a tool. He doesn't get emotional. But then the results speak — sleep, the HiLux, the boots — and that's where the video lands.

One idea: He tested everything. The results are the only proof that counts.

How To Film This

Clothing

Wear light-coloured pants and a light-coloured shirt. Not dark. The belt is dark, and it needs to be visible against your clothing in every shot.

Think: light grey, khaki, cream, stone. No black jeans, no dark tops. Work gear is fine if it's light-coloured.

The Shape Of The Video

The first thing we see: The belt in your hands, turning it over. Or a shot of the inside of the belt — the LEDs, the panels. NOT your face first. The product is the hook object, your voice comes in over the top.

Two rules for the opening:

  • Don't start on your face. Start on the thing, or on your hands examining it
  • Don't say hello and don't introduce yourself. Come in as though we've walked in halfway through

Setup (~20%): The surgery hook. You're a skeptic. You checked the company first. Quick, direct, no fluff.

Product walkthrough (~15%): This is the detailed part. Walk through all four therapies with wavelengths. Show the LEDs, the glow, the settings. Point at things. This is the "I tested it" section. Mention the meter if you have one — if not, talk about the research.

The results (~65%): "Mechanically, it checks out. But here's what actually matters." Sleep. The HiLux. The boots. Stack these moments. Close with "I can test wavelengths all day. But moving like that again? That's the only result that counts."

Tone

Measured, methodical, dry. Like a bloke reviewing a tool he bought from a brand he didn't know. Not a testimonial, not a review — a test report that ended with a surprise. The emotion is understated. He's not excited. He's just... moving again.

Making It Sound Like You And Not Like An Ad

  • Don't act the pain. By now he's used to it. He works around it and carries on. Underplay it every time
  • Slow down. Real people talk slower than actors and leave gaps. If it feels too slow, it's about right
  • Own the skepticism. You're not reading about wavelengths — you've checked them. Talk about the tech like you'd explain a tool to a mate, not like you're quoting a brochure
  • Swap our words for your words. Keep the specifics — the wavelengths, the HiLux, the boots. Change everything around them into how you'd actually say it
  • Keep the first take. Stumbles, restarts, losing your thread — that's what we want. If you nail it cleanly, do another one worse
  • Talk to one person, not a camera. Pick someone you know and tell it to them
  • Don't land the ending. Trail off, shrug, look away. A clean confident finish is what makes something look like a commercial
  • No smiling at the end. That one thing gives it away more than anything else
If you get to the end and it felt a bit flat and unfinished, that's usually the take we want.
