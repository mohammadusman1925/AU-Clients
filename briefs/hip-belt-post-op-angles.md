# Hip Belt — Post-Op Angle Set

Source: Facebook comment, "Hip Pain? Read This." — Heather Campbell.
Waited 2.5 years for a hip replacement. Had it done a year ago. Still can't sleep on that side,
still stiff getting off the lounge or any chair, says it "often feels like an open wound," still in physio.
Been told she needs the other hip done — "no, when hell freezes over, had a rough time after op."
Has a pacemaker, doesn't know if she's allowed to use the belt.

Three separate angles came out of that one comment. Briefs below.

---

Name: Hip Belt - The Surgery Didn't Fix It
Version: V1

Desire: Sleep on that side
Avatar: One year post-op
Persona: Quietly let down
Concept: Nobody warned you
Angle: You did the two-year wait, you did the operation, you're still doing physio — and a year on you still can't lie on that side, and everyone around you acts like you should be over it by now.
Core Message: A new hip doesn't automatically mean a hip that stops hurting — everything around the joint is still angry, and it needs holding, not more waiting.
Level of Awareness: Problem Aware
Funnel: TOFU

Hook 1: The joint they replaced isn't the part that still hurts.
Hook 2: A year post-op and you still can't sleep on that side. You're not the only one, and no, you're not being dramatic.
Hook 3: "It's been a year, you should be fine by now." Come and get me off this lounge then.

Body:
I waited two and a half years for that hip. Two and a half. And when they finally did it last August I honestly thought that was the end of it, that's the hard bit over, now I get my life back. Nobody sat me down and told me what year one actually looks like. I still can't lie on that side. I still have to do the whole production number to get up off the lounge, hands on the arms, count to three, hope nobody's watching. Some days it doesn't even feel like a joint, it feels like an open wound sitting there under the skin. And I'm still turning up to physio like a good girl. You get to a point where you stop telling people because you can see them thinking, she's still going on about that hip. So you just quietly get on with it and take the long way round the chairs. What finally shifted things for me wasn't another exercise. It was something to actually hold the thing. I strap the belt round the hips of a morning, before I've even had a coffee, and it takes the load off the muscles that have been overworking since the day of the op, the ones nobody replaced. First morning I noticed it I got up off the lounge without the count of three. That's it. That's the whole miracle. Sleeping on that side came back slowly after that, not all at once, but it came back. I still don't know why nobody warns you that the surgery is the start of it and not the end.

CTA:
If you're a year out and still doing the count of three, try holding it instead of waiting it out.

---

Name: Hip Belt - When Hell Freezes Over
Version: V1

Desire: Never again
Avatar: Facing hip number two
Persona: Flatly refusing
Concept: Hell freezes over
Angle: They've told you the other hip is next, and after what the first one put you through you'd genuinely rather live in pain than let them do it again.
Core Message: Refusing the second surgery doesn't have to mean signing up for the pain that comes with it.
Level of Awareness: Problem Aware
Funnel: TOFU

Hook 1: She waited two and a half years for her first hip replacement. She's not booking the second one.
Hook 2: If "you'll need the other one done eventually" made your whole stomach drop, this is for you.
Hook 3: I'd rather be in pain than go through that again. And no, I don't want to hear how routine it is.

Body:
The surgeon said it like he was telling me the weather. You'll need the other one done. And I just sat there and thought, no. Not a chance. When hell freezes over. Because he wasn't there for the after. He wasn't there at three in the morning when I couldn't work out how to lie down, he wasn't there for the months of physio, he wasn't there the first time I tried to get off the lounge on my own and had to call out for help like a child. They see the operation. You live the year that follows it. So I made my peace with the other hip hurting, which sounds mad when I say it out loud, but that's genuinely where I got to. Live with it. Just don't touch me again. My daughter is the one who found the belt and I only tried it to get her off my back. It goes round the hips and it holds everything in, and the first thing I noticed wasn't the pain, it was that I trusted the leg again. Stopped bracing before every step. Three weeks in and the bad hip, the one that's supposedly next on the list, is the one giving me the least trouble. I'm not saying I've cured anything. I'm saying nobody's putting me back on that table this year, and for the first time that decision doesn't feel like I'm punishing myself.

CTA:
Not ready for the second one? You don't have to just put up with it in the meantime.

Name: Hip Belt - Nothing To Plug In
Version: V1

Desire: Be allowed to try
Avatar: Ruled out by everything
Persona: Cautiously hopeful
Concept: No battery in it
Angle: You've got a pacemaker, and by now you've trained yourself to scroll straight past every hip gadget online because they all buzz, zap or need charging and none of them are meant for you.
Core Message: This one has no electrical parts at all — it holds the hip, it doesn't send anything through you.
Level of Awareness: Solution Aware
Funnel: MOFU

Hook 1: There's no battery in it. No pads, no charger, no current. It just holds your hip.
Hook 2: Got a pacemaker and you've been scrolling past every one of these? Read the comments on this one.
Hook 3: Half the hip pain fixes online can't be used by the people whose hips are the worst.

Body:
Every single time. You see something for hip pain, you get your hopes up for about four seconds, and then you get to the bit where it says pulses or stimulation or charge it overnight and that's you out. Pacemaker. Next. After a while you stop looking. A woman in the comments the other day asked the same thing I've asked a dozen times, she'd had the hip done, still in agony a year later, said she'd love to try the belt but she's got a pacemaker and didn't know if she was allowed. And she just got left there hanging. So, plainly: there is nothing electrical in this. No battery, no pads, no wires, nothing to charge, nothing going through you at all. It's fabric and compression. It wraps around the hips and holds the joint and the muscles either side of it so they stop overworking every time you stand up. That's the whole mechanism. It's the boring answer and it's the reason it works for people who've been shut out of everything else. If you're not sure, ask your doctor, that's always the right call. But you shouldn't have to sit out of your own recovery because everything on the internet needs a plug.

CTA:
No battery, no current, nothing to interfere with. Have a look and check with your doctor if you're unsure.

## Production notes

- **Angle 1 is the lead.** "The surgery didn't fix it" is the widest and least-served door — the market
  talks to pre-surgery pain constantly and almost never to the year after. The comment section on the
  source post is proof the audience is already sitting there.
- **Angle 2 is the emotional spike.** Higher ceiling, narrower avatar. Best as the follow-up test to
  angle 1, and the strongest of the three for a UGC talking-head read.
- **Angle 3 is a conversion-blocker fix, not a cold angle.** Run it MOFU/retargeting at people who
  engaged and didn't buy. Before it goes live, confirm with the supplier in writing that the product
  contains no electrical or magnetic components, and keep the copy to a factual product statement
  ("no battery, no current") plus "check with your doctor" — do not let it drift into a medical
  safety claim about pacemakers.
- Voice is written AU/UK ("lounge", "physio") to match the source comment. Adjust for other markets.
- The mechanism described is compression/support only. If the actual product does anything else,
  angle 3's body copy needs rewriting before it runs.
