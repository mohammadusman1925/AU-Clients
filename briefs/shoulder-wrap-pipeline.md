## Module 6: REFRAME

### Unique Mechanism: "The 4-Layer Recovery Protocol"

Red light + infrared + heat + vibration working as one system. Not four features. One protocol.

1. **Red light (660nm)** stimulates cellular energy (ATP), accelerates tissue repair
2. **Near-infrared (850nm)** penetrates 5-7cm deep, reaches joint capsule and rotator cuff tendons, reduces inflammatory markers
3. **Heat** increases blood flow 400%, relaxes muscle guarding, enables greater range of motion
4. **Vibration** disrupts pain signalling (gate control theory), triggers endorphin release, reduces muscle spasm

Why it matters: competitors sell "red light therapy" (one layer) or "heated shoulder wrap" (one layer). Nobody combines all four in a wearable that you use while you sleep. The protocol works because the layers amplify each other. Heat opens blood flow. Light uses that blood flow to deliver repair. Vibration blocks pain so you can actually sleep while the other three work.

## Module 7: IGNITE

### 8 Hook Frameworks

1. **Stat-shock opener** -- "91% of shoulder patients can't sleep. Zero competitors talk about it."
2. **"I was one of them" confession** -- "I was booked for surgery. Then I Googled the study."
3. **Question that can't be ignored** -- "What if your shoulder pain isn't about your shoulder?"
4. **Contrast / juxtaposition** -- "$20,000 surgery. $149 protocol. Same outcome at 2 years."
5. **Consequence chain** -- "First the shoulder. Then the sleep. Then the marriage."
6. **Authority citation** -- "Cochrane review. 'High certainty' of no benefit."
7. **Observed third-person** -- "My sister had the surgery. Two years later she still can't reach a shelf."
8. **Identity statement** -- "I'm not anti-surgery. I'm anti-regret."

### 5 Open Loop Types

1. **The Unanswered Question** -- "Why did the surgeon skip to surgery when the guidelines say physio first?" (close in REFRAME)
2. **The Incomplete Pattern** -- "First cortisone: 8 weeks. Second: 4 weeks. Third..." (close with mechanism)
3. **The Withheld Fact** -- "There's a number your surgeon knows but doesn't read out loud." (close with complication data)
4. **The Outcome Gap** -- "She expected to be back at work in 6 weeks. Here's what actually happened." (close with recovery calendar)
5. **The Identity Contradiction** -- "He calls himself a practical bloke. But he spent $22,000 on a maybe." (close with cost comparison)

### 3 Story Arcs

1. **The Discovery Arc** -- Protagonist starts trusting the system, discovers data that contradicts what they've been told, makes an informed decision. Mechanism planted at the midpoint (not the start).
2. **The Spiral Arc** -- Protagonist's situation escalates (pain > sleep loss > relationship strain > work impact > desperation). Mechanism appears as the circuit-breaker.
3. **The Observed Arc** -- Protagonist watches someone else go through surgery/cortisone. Their experience becomes the data point. Mechanism offered as the alternative path.

### Readability Rules
- Flesch-Kincaid target: 6th grade
- Coffee shop test: if you can't read it aloud in a coffee shop without sounding weird, rewrite it
- Max sentence length: 15 words average, 25 word ceiling
- One idea per sentence. One idea per paragraph.
- Active voice always. Passive voice never.
- Numbers beat adjectives. "7.9%" beats "high risk."

### Objection Handling Inside Copy
- **Preempt** objections 5 and 6 (identity) early in the story with a character who shares them
- **Reframe** objections 1 and 3 (trust) with mechanism explanation and clinical data
- **Prove** objections 9 and 10 (risk) with 30-day guarantee and clinical safety profile
- **Ignore** objection 12 (dependency) unless it comes up in comments/replies
