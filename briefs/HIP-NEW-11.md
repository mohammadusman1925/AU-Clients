**2. The Job**

**What this concept tests:** A new angle on daily-limitation pain using
AD 2\'s 19-beat framework. Instead of surgery avoidance (AD 2) or failed
products (HIP-NEW-16), this concept enters through the SMALLEST moment
of pain \-- putting on a sock. The hypothesis is that micro-humiliation
(can\'t do a 5-second task) creates stronger identification than
macro-pain (surgery, can\'t walk) because EVERYONE who has hip pain has
this exact moment but nobody talks about it.

**Hypothesis:** A reader who sees \"ten minutes to put on a sock\" will
experience immediate, visceral identification because it\'s their secret
too. The specificity of the moment (not \"daily pain\" but \"one sock,
ten minutes\") creates a scroll-stop that broader pain claims can\'t
match. If the reader has ever sat on the edge of a bed struggling with a
sock, this ad speaks directly to them in a way no other ad has.

**3. Who\'s Reading**

**Target:** The Silently Struggling Daily Sufferer

A woman (or man) aged 50-70 who has NOT had the surgery conversation
yet. Their pain hasn\'t reached crisis level \-- it\'s reached EROSION
level. Every day, dozens of tiny things take longer, hurt more, or
require workarounds. They haven\'t told anyone the full extent because
the individual moments sound trivial. \"I can\'t put my sock on\" sounds
ridiculous. So they stay quiet and compensate.

**Emotional state:** Quietly defeated but not desperate. They\'ve
accepted this as their new normal. They don\'t search for solutions
anymore because no single moment is bad enough to justify action.
They\'re not in crisis \-- they\'re in slow surrender.

**Key difference from AD 2\'s reader:** AD 2\'s reader is facing surgery
\-- a CRISIS. This reader hasn\'t reached crisis. They\'re in the
erosion phase, where pain steals capability one small task at a time.
They don\'t know they need help because each stolen task feels too small
to count.

**Funnel:** Top of funnel, new customer. Problem-aware but in denial
about the severity.

**4. The Angle**

**Pain cluster:** Daily limitation \-- the invisible tax of chronic hip
pain on the smallest movements

**Specific pain in customer language:** *\"It\'s not even the walking.
It\'s the sock. It\'s the shoe. It\'s getting out of the car. It\'s
every little thing that used to take two seconds.\"*

**What makes this angle different:** AD 2 is about the BIG pain
(surgery). HIP-NEW-16 is about FAILED solutions. This concept is about
the SMALL pain that nobody talks about \-- the accumulated
micro-humiliations that add up to a life that\'s quietly shrinking.

**5. Three Headline Options**

**Option A \-- \"The Sock\"**

*\"It took me ten minutes to put on a sock this morning. Ten minutes.
For one sock.\"*

**Hook type:** Absurd Specificity

**Emotional job:** The repetition and escalation (ten minutes -\> for
one sock) makes the absurdity undeniable. Anyone who has struggled with
this exact task will feel caught. The specificity is the scroll-stop \--
not \"I have hip pain\" but \"one sock, ten minutes.\"

**Option B \-- \"The Alarm Clock\"**

*\"I set my alarm 45 minutes earlier than I used to. Not because I\'m
more productive. Because everything takes longer now.\"*

**Hook type:** Hidden Confession

**Emotional job:** The misdirect (alarm earlier -\> must be productive)
and the reveal (everything takes longer) mirrors the reader\'s own
hidden adjustment. They\'ve made the same invisible accommodations and
never told anyone. The ad names what they\'ve been hiding.

**Option C \-- \"The List\"**

*\"I made a list of things I can\'t do anymore. I stopped at
twenty-three. Not because I ran out. Because I started crying.\"*

**Hook type:** Emotional Confrontation

**Emotional job:** The act of listing creates forced awareness. The
specific number (twenty-three) feels real. The crying isn\'t dramatic
\-- it\'s the quiet breakdown of someone who finally counted the cost.
The reader who has never made their own list will start making one while
reading.

**6. Body Direction**

**Story Shape:** Linear narrative, same structure as AD 2. \~1,400-2,200
words. First-person patient narrator.

**Key Scenes to Build**

**The Sock Scene (Opening):** The opening scene. Detailed, visceral,
specific. Sitting on the edge of the bed. The system she\'s developed.
Foot on opposite knee. The lean. The reach. The moment her hip says no.
The breathing through it. The time it takes. The exhaustion before the
day has started.

**The Accumulation:** Not just the sock \-- the shoe, the car door, the
stairs, reaching for something on a low shelf, getting off the toilet.
Each one a small defeat. Each one taking longer than it should. The list
grows but nobody sees it because each item is too small to mention.

**The Silence:** She doesn\'t tell anyone. What would she say? \"I
can\'t put my socks on\"? It sounds absurd. So she compensates quietly.
Slip-on shoes. Elastic laces. The grabber tool she ordered online and
hides when people visit.

**The Discovery:** She encounters the belt NOT through searching (she\'s
stopped searching for the big solution) but through someone who notices
her struggling with something small. A friend, a family member, someone
who says \"I used to do that exact same thing.\"

**The Mechanism:** Delivered through understanding why her range of
motion shrank \-- not bone, not age, but starved soft tissue creating
restriction.

**The Product:** Natural discovery through the person who noticed.

**The Results:** The first morning she reaches her foot without the
system. Without the breathing. Without the ten minutes. The sock just
goes on.

**The Echo:** She doesn\'t set her alarm early anymore.

**7. The Mechanism**

**Name:** \"Deep Tissue Reset\"

Same mechanism as AD 2, delivered through the lens of daily movement
restriction.

**Current belief:** *\"This is just what getting older feels like. I
have to live with it.\"*

**Reframe:** The reason she can\'t reach her foot isn\'t age. It\'s
inflamed soft tissue around the hip joint that has tightened and
restricted her range of motion. The tissue is starved of blood supply.

**Why nothing worked:** Everything she\'s tried treats symptoms, not the
tissue restriction. Stretching pulls against angry tissue. Pain relief
masks it. Physio exercises load it.

**What actually works:** Driving warmth and fresh blood directly into
the starved tissue to break the inflammation cycle and restore range of
motion.

**New belief:** *\"It wasn\'t age. It was tissue nobody was treating.
And now I can reach my foot again.\"*

**9. Persona & Voice**

New character. NOT Maggie.

**Age:** 54-62

**Voice:** Quieter than HIP-NEW-01\'s narrator. Less determined, more
reflective. She sounds like someone who was so busy compensating she
forgot to be angry. Not resigned \-- surprised. Surprised at how much
she\'d given up without noticing.

**Credential:** *\"I\'m not disabled. I\'m not in a wheelchair. I just
can\'t put my own socks on.\"*

**Tone:** Conversational, intimate, slightly bewildered. She\'s telling
you something she hasn\'t told anyone. Not because it\'s dramatic \--
because it\'s embarrassing. The vulnerability is in the smallness of the
confession, not the size of the pain.

**Key constraint:** No dramatics. No \"I was at my breaking point.\"
This narrator\'s pain is defined by its quietness. The emotion comes
from accumulation, not explosion.
