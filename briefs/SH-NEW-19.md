**2. The Job**

**What this concept tests:**

A new angle on shoulder pain through involuntary movement restriction.
The body overriding the brain. Instead of \"my shoulder hurts\"
(sensation) or \"my partner noticed\" (relationship), this concept
enters through AUTONOMY LOSS. The shoulder is making decisions the
reader didn\'t authorize. The seatbelt moment is universal, specific,
and deeply frustrating because it happens in front of other people
(passengers, family) and reveals a limitation the reader has been
managing privately.

This tests whether the same emotional architecture that works for
chronic-pain storytelling and being-observed storytelling can convert
when the entry point is the body\'s VETO POWER. The reader isn\'t in
pain all the time. The reader isn\'t being watched. The reader is being
ambushed. The shoulder waits until a routine movement and then refuses.
The unpredictability is the psychological hook.

**Hypothesis:**

A reader whose shoulder has \"grabbed\" during a seatbelt reach, or any
routine across-body movement, will recognize this immediately because
it\'s the most AMBUSH-like experience of shoulder pain. There\'s no
warning, no buildup, no time to brace. The shoulder just vetoes the
movement mid-reach. If the reader has ever twisted their whole body to
compensate for a seatbelt reach, this ad catches them in the act. The
conditional, unpredictable nature of the pain creates a different
psychological experience from constant pain: hypervigilance around
ordinary movements. If the beat structure holds, this concept should
open a new creative angle that targets a segment AD 2 and SH-NEW-20 miss
entirely.

**3. Who\'s Reading**

**Target:** The Ambushed Mover

A woman (or man) aged 45-70 whose shoulder pain is not constant but
CONDITIONAL. It appears in specific movements, especially across-body
reaches. They\'re fine sitting still. They\'re fine with arms at their
sides. But the moment they reach across, behind, or above, the shoulder
reminds them it\'s in charge. They\'ve learned to avoid these movements
or substitute alternative paths (left hand for right-side seatbelt,
lower the visor before reaching for it).

**Emotional state when they see this ad:** Frustrated and startled. Each
grab is a mini-ambush. They know it\'s coming eventually but never quite
when. The unpredictability creates anxiety around movement. They start
pre-planning movements to avoid the grab. There\'s a background hum of
vigilance during ordinary tasks that no one else can see.

**Key difference from AD 2\'s reader:** AD 2\'s reader is in CHRONIC
pain. SH-NEW-20\'s reader is BEING WATCHED. This reader is being
AMBUSHED. The pain isn\'t constant. It\'s conditional and unpredictable,
which makes it a different psychological experience: hypervigilance
around ordinary movements.

**Where they are in the funnel:** Top of funnel. Problem-aware but
framing it as \"just my shoulder acting up\" rather than a treatable
condition. They haven\'t sought serious treatment because the pain
isn\'t constant enough to justify it. It only appears when they move a
certain way, so they\'ve rationalized it as manageable.

**4. The Angle**

**Pain cluster:** Involuntary movement restriction. The body vetoing
ordinary movements.

**Specific pain in customer language:** *\"I reached for the seatbelt
and my shoulder just grabbed. Like a wall. I had to twist my whole body
to clip it in.\"*

**What makes this angle different:**

AD 2 is about constant pain. SH-NEW-20 is about being seen in pain. THIS
concept is about SURPRISE pain. The body making decisions without
permission. The seatbelt is the entry point because it\'s a movement
done in front of others (passengers) that reveals a limitation the
reader has been privately managing. The angle works because the reader
doesn\'t think of themselves as someone with a \"shoulder problem.\"
They think of themselves as someone who has to be careful reaching for
things. The reframe is that \"careful\" is the problem.

**5. Three Headline Options**

**Option A - \"The Seatbelt\"**

> *\"I reached for the seatbelt and my shoulder decided for me. It said
> no before I even felt pain.\"*

**Hook type:** Personification + Autonomy Loss

**Emotional job:** Giving the shoulder agency (\"decided for me,\" \"it
said no\") captures exactly how it feels. The body overriding the brain.
\"Before I even felt pain\" is key. It\'s not about pain, it\'s about
CONTROL. The reader whose shoulder has vetoed a movement will feel the
loss of autonomy immediately. The seatbelt is universally recognizable
as a thoughtless, automatic movement, which makes the veto feel more
invasive.

**Option B - \"The Jacket\"**

> *\"I tried to put my jacket on this morning. My right arm went in
> fine. My left arm had to be negotiated with.\"*

**Hook type:** Dark Humor + Specificity

**Emotional job:** \"Negotiated with\" personifies the shoulder while
capturing the reader\'s coping mechanism. They talk to their own body,
bargain with it, work around it. The asymmetry (right fine, left
negotiated) mirrors the reader\'s daily reality. Dark humor makes it
shareable. The reader who has ever had to talk themselves through
putting on a jacket will stop scrolling.

**Option C - \"The Back Seat\"**

> *\"My daughter asked me to pass her bag from the back seat. I had to
> get out of the car and walk around.\"*

**Hook type:** Public Humiliation + Practical Impact

**Emotional job:** The audience (daughter) makes the limitation visible
and undeniable. \"Had to get out and walk around\" shows the absurd
length of the workaround. A 3-second reach replaced by a 30-second
detour. The reader who has done something similar (or avoided the
situation entirely) feels caught. The gap between what the movement
should cost and what it actually costs is the scroll-stopper.

**6. Body Direction**

**Story Shape**

Linear narrative, first-person. \~1,400-2,200 words. Same structure as
AD 2 adapted for the seatbelt-grab angle. The narrator tells her own
story. The discovery figure is either a physio, a friend, or Dr. Sime\'s
content (not named directly). The narrator is the patient, never the
expert.

**Key Scenes to Build**

**The Seatbelt Moment**

The opening scene. Getting in the car. The automatic reach across. The
wall. The grab. The twist. The passenger who doesn\'t notice (or does).
The moment of frustration that\'s too small to explain but too real to
ignore. This is the scroll-stopper. It must feel like a camera placed
inside the car at the exact moment the shoulder says no.

**The Catalogue of Vetoes**

The seatbelt is just one. The jacket. The thing behind her on the back
seat. The bra clasp. The top shelf. Reaching across to pull the door
shut. Every across-body movement is now a negotiation. The list builds
quietly, each item adding weight. The reader starts counting their own
vetoes.

**The Pre-Planning**

She\'s started anticipating. Putting the seatbelt on before the car
moves. Stepping into jackets instead of swinging them on. Moving things
from the top shelf to the lower one. She\'s rearranging her life around
her shoulder\'s veto power. She hasn\'t told anyone she\'s doing this.
She\'s just quietly reorganized her world to avoid the grab.

**The Discovery**

She encounters the belt through someone who recognizes the movement
pattern. Possibly a physio, a friend, or through Dr. Sime\'s content
about why the shoulder catches. The discovery must feel accidental and
credible. She wasn\'t looking for a solution because she didn\'t think
she had a real problem. She just had a shoulder that \"acted up.\"

**The Mechanism**

Delivered through understanding WHY the shoulder grabs. Not injury, not
wear, but starved tissue creating restriction. The rotator cuff tendons
have become starved of blood supply. The tissue is stuck in an
inflammation loop that tightens the joint. The explanation reframes her
entire experience: the grab isn\'t a warning to be careful, it\'s
inflamed tissue physically restricting the joint.

**The Product**

Natural discovery. She finds the belt through the explanation of what
the tissue actually needs: warmth and fresh blood driven directly into
the starved tissue. Red light at 660nm and near-infrared at 850nm,
combined with heat and vibration. The specs feel earned because the
mechanism created the framework for understanding them.

**The Results**

The first seatbelt reach without the grab. The first jacket swing. The
first back-seat reach. Each one a small reclamation of control. The
results mirror the crisis scenes in reverse. Add timeline markers: \"by
the end of week two\" or \"on day eleven.\" Physical and specific, not
abstract.

**The Echo**

She reaches for the seatbelt. It clicks. She doesn\'t even notice.
That\'s the whole point. The movement that used to require a strategy is
invisible again. The echo must land on the absence of the problem, not
the presence of the solution.

**7. The Mechanism**

**Name:** \"Deep Tissue Reset\" (shoulder)

**The Reframe Chain**

**Current belief:** *\"My shoulder catches when I reach across. I just
need to be more careful.\"*

**Reframe:** The \"catch\" isn\'t a warning to be careful. It\'s
inflamed soft tissue physically restricting the joint\'s range of
motion. The rotator cuff tendons have become starved of blood supply.
The tissue is stuck in an inflammation loop that tightens the joint.
Being careful isn\'t managing the problem. It\'s accommodating a tissue
that\'s slowly losing circulation.

**Why nothing worked:** Anti-inflammatories reduce the alarm signal but
don\'t restore range of motion. Physio tries to force range against
restricted tissue. Cortisone numbs it but the restriction returns.
Stretching pulls against tissue that\'s already angry. Every standard
approach either targets the symptom (pain) or fights the restriction
(forced range) without addressing the root cause: tissue that\'s starved
of blood supply and stuck in an inflammation cycle.

**What actually works:** Driving warmth and fresh blood directly into
the starved tissue to break the inflammation cycle, restore blood
supply, and allow the joint to move freely again. Red light (660nm) and
near-infrared (850nm) penetrate to the tissue layer where the
inflammation lives. Heat increases local circulation. Vibration
stimulates blood flow. Compression holds everything against the joint.

**New belief:** *\"My shoulder wasn\'t breaking down. It was starved and
stuck. Now it reaches where I tell it to.\"*

**Delivery**

The mechanism is delivered through understanding the grab itself. The
narrator learns why her shoulder catches, and the explanation reframes
her entire experience. The discovery figure (physio, friend, or Dr.
Sime\'s content) explains what\'s happening inside the joint in terms
the narrator can translate for the reader. The reader should finish the
mechanism section thinking: \"That\'s exactly what mine does. I had no
idea that was why.\"

**9. Persona & Voice**

**Narrator: New Patient Character**

This is a new character. She is NOT Maggie. She is NOT SH-NEW-20\'s
narrator. She needs her own name, her own details, her own voice.

**Suggested profile (copywriter can adjust):**

**Age:** 50-63

**Situation:** An active, capable woman whose shoulder has started
vetoing ordinary movements. She\'s not in chronic pain. She\'s being
ambushed by conditional restriction. She hasn\'t sought serious
treatment because the pain isn\'t constant enough to justify it. She\'s
been managing it privately through workarounds and avoidance strategies.

**Voice:** More assertive than other narrators. She\'s not sad. She\'s
annoyed. Her shoulder is INCONVENIENCING her. The tone is a woman who is
fed up with negotiating with her own body. Not weepy. Sharp. She sounds
like someone who would tell you the truth over coffee and expect you to
do something about it. Dry humor. Impatient with excuses, including her
own.

**Credential:** Peer. \"I\'m not a doctor. I\'m a woman who got tired of
using the wrong hand for everything and twisting her body into shapes
just to clip a seatbelt.\"

**The Discovery Figure**

The discovery figure is this concept\'s equivalent of Denise in AD 2.
He/she delivers the mechanism and challenges the standard approach.

**Maps to:** Dr. David Sime on the landing page (22 years experience).
The ad should not name \"Dr. Sime\" (that\'s the LP\'s reveal), but the
details should align so the LP feels like meeting the person the ad
referenced.

**Role:** Provides the explanation for why the shoulder grabs (starved
tissue restricting the joint), delivers the mechanism (soft tissue vs.
joint), and explains why standard treatments fail.

**How the narrator encounters them:** Flexible. Article, video, post,
physio appointment, friend\'s recommendation. Must feel accidental, not
staged.

**Voice Rules**

First person throughout (patient narrator). The discovery figure\'s
words appear as reported speech or paraphrase.

No medical jargon the narrator wouldn\'t use. When the figure explains
something technical, the narrator translates: \"He said something about
blood supply dropping and tissue starving. What I understood was: the
tendons in my shoulder had stopped getting what they needed, and
everything I\'d tried couldn\'t reach them.\"

Short sentences in emotional moments. Longer sentences when processing
the explanation.

No exclamation marks. No ALL CAPS. No sales language.

The tone is a woman who is annoyed that she spent months negotiating
with her own body when the problem had a name and a solution. She\'s
sharing because she doesn\'t want other people wasting time on
workarounds.
