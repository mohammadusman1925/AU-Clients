## Data Sections (13 total)

### 1. Recovery -- What You Can't Do After Shoulder Surgery
- 29 days before driving again
- Need help showering/dressing for weeks
- Sling for 2-6 weeks
- Manual workers off 3-9 months
- Physio 2-3x/week for 6-12 weeks ($2,000-$6,000)
- Full recovery: 3-12 months
- Sources: McClelland et al. (2005), ANZ Journal of Surgery; Royal Orthopaedic Hospital Birmingham; Cleveland Clinic

### 2. Complications -- What Can Go Wrong
- 7.9% self-reported complication rate (Manderle et al. 2019, JSES)
- Post-op stiffness: 6.4-23.2%
- Infection: 0.16-1.9%, C. acnes in 27% of deep tissue
- Nerve damage: 1-2%
- Serious adverse events at 90 days: 1.2% overall, 1.7% frozen shoulder
- Sources: Manderle et al. (2019), JSES; Patel et al. (2022), BMJ

### 3. Evidence -- Surgery No Better Than Sham
- FIMPACT trial: 0 benefit over sham at 2, 5, 10 years (BMJ)
- Cochrane review: "high certainty" no clinically important benefits
- Failure rate: 10-30%
- Frozen shoulder MUA recurrence: 17.8%
- Exercise therapy = surgery at every timepoint
- Sources: Paavola et al. (2018-2025), BMJ; Karjalainen et al. (2019), Cochrane

### 4. Cortisone Reality -- The Injection That Stops Working
- 72% recurrence vs 8% physio (Coombes, Lancet 2010)
- Relief lasts 6-8 weeks only
- Double cartilage loss, zero pain benefit (McAlindon, JAMA 2017)
- Weakens tendons (Puzzitiello 2020)
- GRASP trial: no long-term benefit (Lancet 2021)
- Max 3-4 injections/year
- $80-250 AUD per injection
- Sources: Coombes et al. (2010), Lancet; McAlindon et al. (2017), JAMA; Hopewell et al. (2021), Lancet

### 5. Cost -- The Real Price Tag
- $18,000-$20,000+ private (uninsured)
- Out-of-pocket gap with insurance: $680-$3,000
- Physio: $2,000-$6,000+
- Lost income: 1-9 months
- If surgery fails (10-30%): do it all again
- Sources: iSelect; CompareTheMarket; Medical Costs Finder (AU Govt)

### 6. Wait Times -- Getting Worse While You Wait
- 14% of ortho patients wait >1 year (AIHW)
- National median: 45 days
- 71.6% of frozen shoulder patients develop anxiety/depression
- Sources: AIHW 2024-25; ScienceDirect (2025)

### 7. Regret -- Not Everyone Is Happy
- 50% still have pain/stiffness at 7 years (Shaffer et al. 1992)
- 60% have measurable restriction years later
- 19-25% dissatisfied after decompression
- Only 39% achieve full recovery from frozen shoulder
- Sources: Shaffer et al. (1992), JBJS; Arthroscopy journal (1999)

### 8. Forever -- Permanent Restrictions
- Max 11kg overhead lift after replacement
- No tennis, racquetball, freestyle/butterfly swimming
- No golf swings with surgical arm
- Sources: Cleveland Clinic; IU Health

### 9. Guidelines -- What Doctors Should Do First
- Exercise therapy = strong first-line recommendation (JOSPT 2020)
- 89.3% improve with conservative treatment
- At least 3 months physio before surgery considered
- Sources: JOSPT (2020); Cochrane (2019); BMJ FIMPACT

### 10. Can't Sleep -- The Night Pain Trap
- 91% report nocturnal pain (Cho et al. 2015, JSES)
- 88.4% report poor sleep preoperatively (Feltri et al. 2025)
- Frozen shoulder = worst sleep quality, PSQI 12.07 vs normal 2-4
- Sleep disruption = #1 reason for surgery referral (Longo 2022)
- Poor sleep increases pain sensitivity 126% (Krause & Walker 2019)
- 67% sleep on painful side (Zenian 2010)
- 73.8% say sleeping on affected side = #1 recovery marker
- Sources: Cho et al. (2015), JSES; Feltri et al. (2025), KSSTA; Krause & Walker (2019), J Neuroscience; Seron et al. (2023), BMC MSK

### 11. Pre-Diabetic -- The Connection Nobody Tells You About
- 83.5% of frozen shoulder patients are diabetic/pre-diabetic (Pandey et al. 2023, JSES Int)
- 84.7% of pre-diabetic patients missed by standard tests
- 2 million Australians are pre-diabetic (Diabetes Australia 2024)
- 4.38x higher risk with T2DM (Dyer et al. 2025)
- Cortisone spikes blood sugar 8-10+ days in diabetics
- Cortisone triggered ICU admission for DKA (Pabani et al. 2025)
- 36% repeat surgery rate for diabetics vs 15% (Jenkins et al. 2012)
- 38% increased repeat MUA risk for type 1 diabetics (Woods & Loganathan 2017)
- Frozen shoulder may warn of undiagnosed diabetes (Dyer et al. 2024)
- Sources: Pandey et al. (2023), JSES International; Dyer et al. (2025), BMC MSK; Safran et al. (2022), JSES Int; Diabetes Australia (2024)

### 12. Frozen Shoulder -- Condition Profile
- Only 39% achieve full recovery
- Average duration: 30.1 months
- 50% still have pain at 7 years
- 60% have measurable restriction years later
- 71.6% develop anxiety/depression
- MUA recurrence: 17.8%
- 89.3% improve with conservative treatment
- Most common: women aged 40-60
- Sources: Shaffer et al. (1992), JBJS; Woods & Loganathan (2017), BJJ; Zreik et al. (2016)

### 13. Shoulder Bursitis -- Condition Profile

- 10-30% failure rate
- 19-25% dissatisfied
- Exercise therapy = surgery at every timepoint

- Sources: FIMPACT (BMJ); Cochrane (2019); GRASP (Lancet 2021)
