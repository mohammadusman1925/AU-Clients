## Data Sections

### [RECOVERY] What You Can't Do After Hip Surgery

**14 weeks of restrictions** -- minimum. That's if surgery goes perfectly. No complications. No setbacks. This is the *best case.*

- **Can't bend past 90 degrees** for 6-12 weeks. Can't pick anything off the floor. Can't lean forward to reach.
- **Can't tie your own shoes** for 6-8 weeks. Need a sock aid, long shoe horn, and dressing stick.
- **Can't drive** for 2-6 weeks (depends on which hip). No work commute. No school runs. No independence.
- **Can't cross your legs** -- dislocation risk. The thing you do without thinking 50 times a day? Gone.
- **Can't sleep on your side** for weeks. Need a pillow between your knees. The position that doesn't hurt? Banned.
- **Need a raised toilet seat.** Can't use a normal toilet for weeks because you can't bend that far.
- **Can't work for 4-6 weeks** minimum. Desk job: 4 weeks. Physical job: 3-6 months.
- **Can't shower normally.** Need a shower chair and handheld showerhead. Someone may need to help you.

> *Sources: University of Utah Health hip replacement patient guide; EmergeOrtho recovery guidelines; Hofmann Arthritis Institute discharge instructions*

---

### [COMPLICATIONS] What Can Go Wrong

**6.6% infection rate** in one Australian study of hip replacements. That's 1 in 15 patients. Infection means emergency surgery, IV antibiotics, weeks of treatment -- sometimes the prosthesis has to come out entirely.

- **Dislocation: 1-3%** of hip replacements dislocate. The ball pops out of the socket. Requires emergency treatment, sometimes second surgery. Rate jumps to **28% for revision surgery.**
- **Blood clots (DVT): 3%** of orthopaedic surgical patients develop DVT even WITH prevention. Without preventive treatment, up to **80% would develop DVT.**
- **Pulmonary embolism: 1.5%** of orthopaedic patients. If you get a PE, your in-hospital death rate is **2.6%** -- 26x higher than patients without one.
- **Nerve damage:** can cause numbness, tingling, or weakness in the leg. Sometimes permanent.
- **Leg length difference:** one leg ends up slightly longer or shorter than the other. May need a shoe lift forever.
- **DVT and PE are the #1 cause** of emergency readmission after joint replacement surgery.

> *Sources: Bone & Joint NSW study; Drugwatch complication data; Physiopedia THA complications; CDC venous thromboembolism data; NCBI 1.6M patient mortality study*

---

### [REVISION] The Second Surgery Nobody Mentions

**35% lifetime revision risk** for men who get hip replacement in their early 50s. That's a 1-in-3 chance of going through the entire thing again -- except revision surgery is harder, riskier, and has a **28% dislocation rate.**

- **Hip implants last 15-25 years.** If you're 45, you'll likely need at least one revision in your lifetime. Maybe two.
- **Under-50s have DOUBLE the revision risk** compared to patients 65+. Younger patients are more active, which wears the prosthesis faster.
- **20-year implant survival: 85%.** That means 15% of hip replacements fail within 20 years.
- **Median time to revision for under-60s: 4.4 years.** Not decades. Years.
- **Revision surgery complications are worse:** dislocation rate jumps from 1-3% to 28%. Infection rate higher. Recovery longer.
- **AOANJRR data:** 9.6% of all hip procedures reported were revisions in 2015.

> *Sources: AOANJRR 2016/2025 annual reports; Lancet population-based cohort study; Dutch Arthroplasty Register; Franciscan Health younger patient data*

### [COST] The Real Price Tag

**$4,800 average out-of-pocket cost** for Australians who have private health insurance. That's AFTER insurance pays its part. 85% of insured patients still pay out of pocket.

- **Private hip replacement total: $18,000-$30,000.** Surgeon fees (~$5,200) + anaesthetist + hospital accommodation + theatre + prosthesis.
- **85% of insured patients pay out-of-pocket** -- average $4,800 gap. "Restricted benefit" policies cover almost nothing.
- **Self-funded (no insurance): $20,000-$30,000** total out of pocket.
- **Gap fees rising 7.7% per year** (APRA 2025 data). Getting more expensive every year.
- **Hidden costs nobody quotes:** 6 weeks off work (lost income), equipment (shower chair, raised toilet seat, grabber tools), physio (6-12 months), follow-up appointments, medication.
- **If you need revision surgery:** do it all again. Another $20K-$30K. More time off work. More hidden costs.

> *Sources: Canstar 2025 hip replacement cost guide; CompareTheMarket; CompareClub; APRA gap data 2025; Dr Sam Martin hip replacement cost breakdown*

### [WAIT] 152 Days Of Getting Worse

**152 days -- median public hospital wait** for hip replacement in Australia (2024-25 AIHW data). That's 5 months. And **84% of patients get worse** while they wait.

- **Public hospital median: 152 days** (improved from 175 days in 2022-23, but still 5 months)
- **Private hospital median: 28 days** -- but costs $20K-$30K
- **84% of patients deteriorate** while waiting for surgery. Pain gets worse. Mobility decreases. Mental health declines.
- **1 in 5 patients** describe waiting pain as "worse than anything they've ever experienced"

> *Sources: AIHW MyHospitals 2024-25; Health Services Daily; VOC research (200+ forum posts)*

### [REGRET] Not Everyone Is Happy They Did It

**9% still in pain at long-term follow-up.** Nearly 1 in 10 people go through the surgery -- the recovery, the restrictions, the cost -- and still have persistent pain.

- **7-15% of patients are dissatisfied** after total hip replacement
- **5-13% experience decision regret** -- they wish they hadn't done it
- **8.2% dissatisfied at 5 years** -- the number doesn't improve much over time
- **9% have persistent pain** at long-term follow-up. The surgery was supposed to fix the pain. It didn't.
- That means roughly **1 in 10 people** go through an irreversible, $20K+ surgery and either regret it or still have pain

> *Sources: ScienceDirect decision regret study; NCBI satisfaction study of 850 patients; Arthroplasty Journal 5-year outcome study; Orthopaedic Reviews*

### [FOREVER] What You Can Never Do Again

**0 high-impact activities allowed -- ever.** No running. No jumping. No martial arts. No football. No rugby. No ice hockey. No boxing. These restrictions don't end at 14 weeks. They end when you do.

- **No running or jogging** -- permanently. Forces several times body weight across the joint.
- **No jumping** -- any activity involving impact landing is permanently restricted.
- **No contact sports** -- rugby, football, ice hockey, martial arts, boxing are all off the table forever.
- **No deep squatting** -- extreme flexion risks dislocation (5% dislocation risk with smaller bearings).
- **Heavy lifting restricted** -- permanent, varies by surgeon recommendation.
- **Allowed:** walking, cycling, swimming, golf, doubles tennis, moderate skiing. That's the approved list.

> *Sources: Biology Insights permanent restrictions; Healthline lifetime precautions; Penn State Bone & Joint life after replacement; Cory Calendine MD*

### [GUIDELINES] What Doctors Are Supposed To Do First

- **Medical guidelines say: try conservative treatment FIRST.** National and international evidence-based guidelines for hip OA recommend starting with conservative treatments, followed by surgery only if conservative options don't work.
- **"Important to delay primary THA by optimizing conservative treatment, especially in young people"** -- this is from the medical literature itself. Surgeons who skip straight to surgery are going against guidelines.
- **Non-pharmacological treatments are underutilised.** Australian GPs are not giving non-drug options the importance that clinical practice guidelines suggest.
- **The RACGP published specific guidelines** for non-surgical management of hip and knee OA. They exist. GPs aren't following them.

> *Sources: RACGP osteoarthritis clinical practice guideline; NCBI non-compliance study; ScienceDirect conservative treatment for hip OA; Australian GP management study*
